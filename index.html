The problem

For many people, going to an event in Parramatta means guessing whether they can actually get in, move around and take part.

The people most affected are wheelchair users and people with mobility needs, people who are blind or have low vision, Deaf and hard-of-hearing people, autistic and sensory-sensitive people, older residents, carers and families, and culturally diverse communities — anyone who needs to know about step-free entrances, accessible toilets, quiet spaces and door widths before they decide to go.

Information is scattered Access details live across listings, maps and social posts — or nowhere at all.
Barriers are found on the day A step, a locked lift or no quiet space is discovered too late, at the event.
Council hears about attendance …but rarely about whether people could navigate, participate and enjoy the event.

How it works

One event, one access plan — in three steps.

Open an event and choose what you need. The plan rebuilds itself around you, and stays honest about what it does not know.

1

Before you leave

Pick your needs — mobility, sensory, family — and see entrances, toilets, quiet spaces and lifts on a real map. Every item is marked verified, requires confirmation, or missing.

2

During the event

The plan works offline, so it still helps when the network drops in a busy laneway. It also supports read-aloud, larger text, dark mode and colour-blind-friendly status colours.

3

After the event

Share a short, voluntary report — could you navigate, participate and enjoy it? Council sees where events work and where they don't, in the community's own words.

Community benefit

Fewer wasted trips, more confidence — and a record Council can act on.

If people can trust the information before they travel, more of them show up. If Council can see the gaps, it can fix them for the next event.

Data & sources

Where the information comes from.

Everything is attributed, and nothing is presented as Council's own operational data.

OpenStreetMap accessibility tags Candidate venue access attributes (wheelchair access, toilets, surfaces). Licence: ODbL.
Transport for NSW Open Data Accessible stops and journey context. Planned static-first, with graceful degradation.
City of Parramatta event listings Event name, date, venue and public description — consumed with attribution, never duplicated.
Major Events and Festivals Strategy 2025–2030 Community engagement facts and event context.
Council strategy & policy documents Disability Inclusion Action Plan, Social Sustainability Strategy, Smart City and Innovation Strategy — source of the readiness checklist.
Synthetic data (clearly labelled) Plausible attendance and resource figures used to demonstrate scenario planning. Not real Council data.
MapLibre + OpenStreetMap tiles Base map for the access plan, via OpenFreeMap. Attribution rendered in the map.

Privacy & responsible AI

AI helps — but a person always decides.

Privacy

No accounts for attendees. No health information collected. The needs you choose stay on your device and are never sent to a server. Community reports are screened for personal information before anything is published.

Tools we used

OpenAI to build the website, and ChatGPT for the HTML design and colour scheme. Inside the product, AI only suggests an access attribute or a risk.

Human check

Nothing AI-suggested is published until a person confirms it. Each item shows its source, when it was last checked, and a confidence that decays over time.

Risks we manage

Stale or incomplete data, over-trust in AI suggestions, and exclusion when data is missing. We mitigate these with provenance, confidence decay, and openly showing what we don't know.

Council's role

We'd ask Council to partner and advocate.

Partner: share event and venue information, and give us access to the spaces so the plan is accurate.

Advocate: encourage venues, organisers and partners to publish accessible information and adopt the practice.

We are not asking Council to deliver or fund a new platform, or to regulate — a small, shared pilot is enough to prove the value.
