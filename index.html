Parramatta Explorer

An accessible plan for every City of Parramatta event, so you know you can get in before you leave home.

For people with disability, older residents and families, deciding whether to go to an event usually means guessing whether they can actually get in, move around and take part.

That affects wheelchair users and people with mobility needs, people who are blind or have low vision, Deaf and hard-of-hearing people, autistic and sensory-sensitive people, older residents, carers and families, and culturally diverse communities.

Community benefit

Fewer wasted trips, more confidence — and a record Council can act on.

If people can trust the information before they travel, more of them turn up. If Council can see the gaps, it can fix them for the next event.

How we'll measure it:

  • the share of events with a verified access plan
  • the number of "unknown" access fields closed over time
  • post-event feedback on whether people could navigate, participate and enjoy the event — not just whether they attended

Data & sources

Where the information comes from

Everything is attributed, and nothing is presented as Council's own operational data.

  • OpenStreetMap accessibility tags (ODbL) — candidate venue access attributes
  • Transport for NSW Open Data — accessible stops and journey context
  • City of Parramatta event listings — event name, date, venue, public description
  • Major Events and Festivals Strategy 2025–2030 — community engagement facts and event context
  • Council strategy & policy documents — Disability Inclusion Action Plan, Social Sustainability Strategy, Smart City and Innovation Strategy
  • Clearly-labelled synthetic data — attendance and resource scenarios (not real Council data)
  • MapLibre + OpenStreetMap tiles — base map, via OpenFreeMap

Privacy & responsible AI

AI helps — but a person always decides

Privacy: no accounts for attendees, no health information collected. The needs you choose stay on your device and are never sent to a server.

Tools we used: OpenAI to build the website, and ChatGPT for the HTML design and colour scheme.

Human check: AI only suggests an access attribute or a risk. Nothing is published until a person confirms it, and each item shows its source and when it was last checked.

Risks we manage: stale or incomplete data, over-trust in AI suggestions, and exclusion when data is missing — handled by showing provenance and openly showing what we don't know.

Council's role

We'd ask Council to partner and advocate

Partner: share event and venue information, and give us access to the spaces so the plan is accurate.

Advocate: encourage venues, organisers and partners to publish accessible information and adopt the practice.

We're not asking Council to deliver or fund a new platform, or to regulate — a small, shared pilot is enough to prove the value.

It fits with Council's plans:

  • Major Events and Festivals Strategy 2025–2030 — Measurement & Evaluation
  • Disability Inclusion Action Plan
  • Smart City and Innovation Strategy
  • Social Sustainability Strategy
